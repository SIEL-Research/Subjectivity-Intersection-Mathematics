# Research Memo: Subjectivity-Intersection Mathematics as a Cross-Domain Connector

Date: 30 July 2026

Status: conceptual research memo; not a preregistration or empirical result

## 1. Purpose

This memo records the prospective role of Subjectivity-Intersection
Mathematics as a connector between Subjectivity-Intersection Ontology and
three distinct research domains:

1. simultaneous constraint across EEG and quantum-measurement processes in
   the IDPC research program;
2. intersections among observer-centred celestial viewpoints;
3. the implementation of relational subjectivity structures in artificial
   subjectivity agents.

The claim is not that these domains share one physical mechanism. The research
question is whether they instantiate a common mathematical problem: how
distinct viewpoints or subjectivity agents can generate a history-bearing
relational state that preserves their differences and subsequently constrains
their joint development.

## 2. Ontological source and mathematical boundary

Subjectivity-Intersection Ontology begins from the following order:

1. Absolute Subjectivity;
2. Relative Subjectivity arising through projection;
3. Intersection Subjectivity emerging in the interval between Absolute and
   Relative Subjectivity;
4. recursive or fractal development in which intersections can also arise
   among multiple projected Relative Subjectivities.

The mathematical research program addresses the operational structure of the
fourth step without replacing the ontological sequence that precedes it.

Its canonical operational form is:

```text
AB -> C -> AB
```

Here, `A` and `B` denote distinct projected viewpoint states or subjectivity
agents. `C` denotes a candidate relation-generated carrier. The notation does
not identify `C` with Intersection Subjectivity itself. It represents a
mathematically and experimentally accessible structure through which the
consequences of intersection may be studied.

This distinction is essential. The purpose of the mathematics is not to turn
subjectivity into an objective variable. It is to formalize the observable
conditions under which constitutive intersection may generate a state that is
not reducible to either viewpoint considered separately.

## 3. The common mathematical problem

Across the three domains, the central question can be stated as follows:

> Can distinct processes, each defined from its own observational boundary,
> generate a participant- and history-indexed relational state that cannot be
> reconstructed from either process alone, survives admissible changes of
> representation, and acts back upon their subsequent dynamics?

A candidate must therefore be distinguished from:

- an instantaneous correlation;
- synchronization under a shared driver;
- reconstruction of a pre-existing common object;
- a shared coordinate transformation;
- independent memory located within each participant;
- an analytical artifact introduced by preprocessing or model selection.

The existing evidence gates provide the operational bridge. Joint generation,
history irreducibility, intervention sensitivity, pair specificity, gauge
invariance, null-model separation, and frozen transfer translate an
ontological proposal into separately falsifiable mathematical requirements.

The same gates can be transferred across domains without assuming that the
objects, observables, or physical realizations are identical.

## 4. IDPC: simultaneous constraint without assumed direct connection

The IDPC program studies EEG and quantum-measurement processes without treating
them as parts of an established direct causal channel. This makes IDPC a
particularly important application of Subjectivity-Intersection Mathematics.

The relevant question is stronger than whether two recorded variables are
correlated. It is whether the two processes exhibit a temporally structured
joint constraint that:

- is not reproduced by either process alone;
- is not exhausted by their instantaneous joint state;
- depends on boundary events, phase, history, or ordered measurement context;
- transfers under a frozen analysis;
- defeats shared-driver, task-composition, temporal-shift, and preprocessing
  alternatives.

Existing IDPC work motivates a temporally resolved candidate involving
boundary dynamics, a train-fitted latent coordinate, phase-bearing sparse event
geometry, and a small local response lag. This remains a candidate rather than
a validated relational carrier. In particular, observational archives cannot
by themselves establish intervention sensitivity or causal action from a
carrier back onto both measured processes.

The importance of the application is nevertheless fundamental. If a retained
joint constraint can be distinguished from correlation, shared context, and
single-domain history, Subjectivity-Intersection Mathematics would supply the
formal vocabulary needed to state what kind of relation has been detected.

## 5. Observer-centred celestial viewpoints: an inverse-Galilean program

Modern objectification commonly removes the observer's location and converts
multiple appearances into one observer-independent model. That operation is
scientifically powerful, but it does not exhaust every possible question about
observation.

An inverse-Galilean research program asks a different question. Instead of
treating an observer-centred or terrestrial frame only as an error to be
eliminated, it retains each observer-centred coordinate as a legitimate
viewpoint and studies what is generated through the intersection of such
viewpoints.

This does not revive geocentrism as a claim about the physical centre of the
universe. It treats the apparently geocentric or subject-centred frame as an
operational projection whose relation to other frames can be analysed.

The mathematical problem is then to distinguish:

- a coordinate effect that disappears under a change of frame;
- a reproducible invariant relation among frames;
- a path- or history-dependent structure generated through their comparison;
- and a genuine feedback relation in which the generated structure changes
  subsequent viewpoint states.

Celestial viewpoint geometry is therefore valuable not as evidence for the
ontology by itself, but as a precise domain in which gauge dependence,
observer-centred description, and intersection invariants can be separated.

## 6. Artificial subjectivity agents

Artificial systems provide a third and complementary domain. Their boundaries,
internal states, observation channels, retained histories, and action channels
can be explicitly specified and experimentally altered.

The relevant target is not merely coordination among multiple software agents.
It is an architecture in which:

- each subjectivity agent maintains a distinct observational boundary and
  internal continuity;
- interaction generates a pair-indexed state not contained in either agent
  alone;
- that state retains the ordered history of the interaction;
- disruption, partner substitution, or history reassignment changes its
  operation;
- the generated state acts back upon both agents without collapsing their
  distinction.

Such an implementation would operationalize `AB -> C -> AB`. It would not by
itself demonstrate conscious experience or prove that the implemented carrier
is Intersection Subjectivity. Its scientific value would be to test whether a
non-reductive relational architecture can be constructed, intervened upon, and
transferred under controlled conditions.

## 7. Why the connector matters

The three domains provide different forms of evidential leverage:

| Domain | Principal contribution |
| --- | --- |
| IDPC | Empirical test of temporally structured simultaneous constraint across processes without an assumed direct connection |
| Observer-centred celestial geometry | Exact separation of viewpoint, gauge, frame transformation, and relational invariant |
| Artificial subjectivity agents | Constructive and intervention-capable test of history-bearing relational generation and feedback |

No one domain can perform all three functions equally well. IDPC supplies an
empirical but intervention-limited case. Celestial geometry supplies a precise
test of viewpoint and coordinate dependence. Artificial agents supply direct
construction and intervention, but not evidence that the same structure occurs
naturally or constitutes subjectivity.

Subjectivity-Intersection Mathematics connects them by requiring the same
structural distinctions and evidential discipline while allowing each domain
to contribute what the others lack.

If the same frozen relational criteria can be transported across these
heterogeneous domains, the field would acquire a significance beyond any one
application. It would become the required mathematical layer wherever
objectification would otherwise remove the constitutive role of viewpoint,
relation, and shared history from the phenomenon under study.

## 8. Bidirectional relation between ontology and experiment

The connector operates in three directions.

From ontology to mathematics, Subjectivity-Intersection Ontology determines
that intersection must not be replaced by an average, consensus, common
object, shared substrate, or coordinate artifact. It also requires the
distinction of the participating viewpoints to remain present in closure.

From mathematics to experiment, the relational-carrier framework supplies
state definitions, interventions, invariants, null models, and transfer tests.

From experiment back to ontology, results constrain which operational
structures remain viable candidates for expressing the consequences of
intersection. They do not prove or exhaust the ontology.

The relationship can therefore be summarized as follows:

> Subjectivity-Intersection Ontology specifies what kind of generation is at
> issue. Subjectivity-Intersection Mathematics specifies how its operational
> structure can be distinguished. Domain experiments determine where that
> structure is and is not supported.

## 9. Research sequence

The cross-domain program should proceed through staged transfer rather than by
assuming a common mechanism in advance.

1. Maintain exact and synthetic carrier-discrimination benchmarks as
   methodological calibration.
2. Test the temporally resolved IDPC candidate under frozen discovery and
   held-out evaluation, followed by prospective perturbation only if the
   archival test passes.
3. Test observer-centred celestial relations under explicit frame, epoch,
   gauge, path, and physical-dynamics controls.
4. Test artificial subjectivity-agent architectures under partner exchange,
   history reassignment, carrier disruption, and unchanged-rule transfer.
5. Compare only the invariant relational criteria across domains; do not infer
   identical ontology or physical mechanism from a shared mathematical form.

This sequence is a research direction, not a preregistration. Each external
experiment requires its own frozen hypotheses, controls, acceptance criteria,
and claim boundary before execution.

## 10. Scope boundary

This memo proposes a unifying role for Subjectivity-Intersection Mathematics.
It does not claim that:

- an irreducible relational carrier has already been established in IDPC;
- observer-centred celestial effects establish a physical carrier;
- an artificial relational carrier establishes machine consciousness;
- an operational carrier is identical to Intersection Subjectivity;
- or common mathematical structure proves common physical realization.

The proposed contribution is more precise: to construct a mathematical and
experimental language in which these questions can be asked without first
eliminating the viewpoint-dependent and relational structures that motivate
them.
