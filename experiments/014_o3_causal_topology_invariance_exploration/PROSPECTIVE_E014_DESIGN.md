# Candidate public E014 — prospective cross-domain transfer design

Status: `LOCAL_DESIGN_DRAFT_NOT_REGISTERED_NOT_EXECUTED`

## Irreducibly cross-domain question

Can the stabilized causal advantage of a correctly returned O3 relation be
fixed from two domains and predict every registered late checkpoint for a new
target in the third domain, without a shared scalar entering any domain engine?

The transferred object is only the causal direction:

```text
after a domain-native stabilization interval:

correct O3 return
  > O3 removal

correct O3 return
  > physically distinguishable mismatched O3 return
```

The comparison sign means “closer to the preregistered domain state under that
domain's own registered readout.” It does not assert common numerical values,
a common O3 substance, or microscopic source identity.

## Fresh prospective targets

The public registration should use targets that have not been executed in the
E014 exploration.

- **Atomic:** a new hydrogenic helium-4 reduced-mass/charge target, with a nonperiodic
  physical translation of the soft-Coulomb centre as the mismatch. The primary
  readout is Hellinger similarity of the full density to matched intact
  dynamics. The first primary checkpoint is at least 200 dynamic steps after
  return. Before reserving this named target, an unnamed synthetic stress grid
  spanning nuclear/electron mass ratios `1`–`10,000` and charges `0.5`–`3.0`
  passed all `120/120` checks; the 200-step-or-later subset passed `90/90` with
  minimum margin `0.130416065`.
- **Molecular:** a freshly calculated `cc-pVTZ` `H4+` geometry line at a fixed
  `b` not present in the exploration (candidate: `b = 1.1 Å`). The complete
  molecule-minus-isolated carrier must be generated before dynamics. The
  primary readout is Hellinger similarity of the full coordinate distribution.
  The primary checkpoint is 100 base return steps, repeated at grid factors
  `1`, `2`, and `4` with factor-squared time scaling.
- **Cellular:** new, nonoverlapping E009 seeds and held-out damage levels between
  the explored levels. Candidate boundary predictions are:
  - damage `0.675`: reinjection at 45 minutes succeeds, reinjection at 50
    minutes fails;
  - damage `0.825`: reinjection at 45 minutes succeeds, reinjection at 50
    minutes fails.
  Stabilized specificity is evaluated from 40 minutes after reinjection onward.
  The primary discrete bracketing rule is fixed without executing either
  reserved amplitude: when the two nearest executed amplitudes have the same
  pass/fail outcome at a registered reinjection minute in every independent
  cohort, assign that outcome to the interior held-out amplitude. For `0.675`,
  neighbours `0.650` and `0.700` both passed at 45 and failed at 50 minutes.
  For `0.825`, neighbours `0.800` and `0.850` did the same. Four independent
  32-lineage cohorts reproduced all 32 neighbour amplitude-time cells exactly.
  Any continuous boundary fit is secondary and cannot replace this frozen
  primary prediction after execution.

Target identifiers, constants, seeds, source hashes, and every checkpoint must
be frozen in a target registry before the preregistration release.

## Domain-prior O3 and mismatch gates

Before any target outcome is inspected:

1. Generate the domain O3 candidate by the registered domain bridge.
2. Verify exact or tolerance-bounded reconstruction of the native generator.
3. Construct a nonperiodic, physically meaningful mismatch.
4. Verify carrier-level structural difference.
5. Verify that the mismatch is distinguishable under the registered domain
   readout at the chosen regime using a pre-outcome resolution rule.
6. Reject any mismatch selected by survival, recovery, or target-score outcome.

## Primary cross-domain gates

For each new target:

1. **Removal necessity:** stabilized correct return is strictly closer to the
   registered domain state than continued O3 removal.
2. **Return specificity:** stabilized correct return is strictly closer than
   the physically distinguishable mismatch.
3. **Late persistence:** both inequalities hold at every registered checkpoint
   at or after the frozen domain stabilization interval.
4. **Representation covariance:** the molecular direction holds at all three
   registered grid factors.
5. **Cellular boundary prediction:** every preregistered pass/fail boundary
   statement is correct on the new seeds.
6. **Three-way leave-one-domain-out transfer:** for each held-out domain, the
   direction fixed by the other two domains matches every held-out primary
   checkpoint.

The overall decision is supported only if all primary gates pass. No common
absolute score threshold is used.

## Uncertainty and nulls

- Atomic and finite-state molecular primary distributions use deterministic or
  exact propagation where available.
- Cellular margins use paired seeds and a preregistered confidence procedure;
  the primary inequality must have a lower confidence bound above zero.
- Condition-label permutation/sign-flip nulls are secondary calibration checks,
  not substitutes for the all-domain conjunction.
- Early, pre-stabilization checkpoints are reported as controls and may cross;
  they are not silently removed.
- JS, total variation, and Wasserstein are reported as secondary readouts. They
  do not replace the registered primary readout after execution.

## Explicit failure conditions

The experiment fails its cross-domain claim if any of the following occurs:

- a correct-return late checkpoint is not strictly above removal or mismatch;
- the direction changes under a registered molecular grid representation;
- a cellular held-out boundary prediction is wrong;
- any leave-one-domain-out fold fails;
- the O3 candidate or mismatch requires result-informed repair;
- a shared scalar is introduced into the domain engines after registration.

Every failure, transient crossing, alternative-readout disagreement, and open
result must be retained.

## Claims allowed by a positive result

A positive result would support:

> The stabilized causal advantage of correctly returned O3 is prospectively
> transferable across independent atomic, molecular, and cellular realizations.
> The commonality is a constitutive causal structure, not a common scalar or
> substance.

It would not establish a common microscopic atom-to-cell law, identical O3
entities across domains, living-cell confirmation, phenomenal subjectivity, or
universal instantaneous recovery-speed dominance.
