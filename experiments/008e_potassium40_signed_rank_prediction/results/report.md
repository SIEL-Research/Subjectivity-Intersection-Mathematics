# Experiment 008E result

Decision: **OPEN_NOVEL_PREDICTION_NO_INDEPENDENT_BENCHMARK**

E008E is complete as a DOI-preregistered open numerical prediction:

- `delta A(4P_1/2; K-40) = 0.008107082333170607 kHz`;
- registered input envelope: `[0.007968190838682242, 0.008247506485579788] kHz`;
- prediction SHA-256: `84aef84d7abb9e692f9b5439f3980dfab76f7ca4cec022f769154141a8c1cc9f`;
- preregistration DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.21855190](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21855190).

The frozen searches were executed in Crossref, Google Scholar, NASA ADS,
arXiv, and OpenAlex as the available citation-index equivalent. Eight plausible
sources were screened. None supplies an independent experimental extraction or
independent electronic-structure value for the complete K-40 `M1-M1 + M1-E2`
correction.

The strongest experimental candidate, the 2006 frequency-comb study
([Phys. Rev. A 74, 032503](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.74.032503)),
reports `A(4P_1/2; K-40) = -34.523(25) MHz`. Its 25 kHz uncertainty cannot
separate the approximately 8 Hz registered correction, and the paper does not
extract that correction. Its explicit second-order adjustment is a Doppler
correction, not the registered hyperfine target.

The 1981 D-line experiment
([J. Phys. B 14, 4231](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3700/14/22/009))
likewise reports an effective `A` constant with about 110 kHz uncertainty, not
the complete correction. Independent calculations of potassium tune-out
wavelengths and nuclear quadrupole moments address different observables.
The Beloy-Derevianko paper is the disclosed construction source and omits K-40
from its correction table, so it is class C rather than an independent target.

No numerical benchmark value was extracted and the registered evaluator was
not invoked. This follows the preregistered no-benchmark rule: the prediction
remains externally testable and is not replaced by K-39, K-41, another state,
or a same-source reconstruction.
